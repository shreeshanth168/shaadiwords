Traditional vs Modern Invitation Wording

There's no universally "correct" style — it depends on your families, your guest list, and what the wedding itself feels like. Here's how to think through it.

Go traditional if...

  • Your parents or elders are heavily involved in planning and expect a formal card
  • The printed invitation will be the primary way older relatives learn about the wedding
  • Your wedding follows customary rituals closely, and you want the wording to reflect that

Traditional wording usually names both families, uses formal phrases like "request the honour of your presence," and follows an established structure guests recognize immediately.

Go modern if...

  • You and your partner are the primary point of contact for most guests (rather than parents)
  • Most invites will go out digitally — WhatsApp, Instagram, e-invites — rather than printed cards
  • You want the invitation to reflect your personalities, not just wedding convention

Modern wording tends to be shorter, first-person ("We're getting married!"), and skips the formal family-name preamble.

The common middle ground

Many couples in India today do both: a traditional printed card for the older generation and close relatives, and a modern, casual message for the WhatsApp/Instagram audience. This way, nobody feels like the tone is "wrong" for them.
