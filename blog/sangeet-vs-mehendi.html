Sangeet vs Mehendi: What's the Difference?

Both are pre-wedding celebrations, and both often happen close together — which is exactly why people mix up their invitation wording. Here's a simple breakdown.

Mehendi

The Mehendi ceremony is where henna (mehendi) is applied to the bride's (and often guests') hands and feet in intricate designs. It's traditionally a daytime, women-centric event, though modern celebrations often include everyone. The mood is relaxed, colorful, and artistic — think marigold decor, folk music, and henna artists at work.

Sangeet

Sangeet literally means "sung together." It's a music and dance-focused celebration where both families perform choreographed dances, sing, and celebrate the union — usually held in the evening, often the night before the wedding. It's high-energy, with a stage, DJ or live band, and a lot of rehearsed dance performances from family and friends.

Why this matters for invitations

Your wording should match the vibe: Mehendi invites often mention henna, colors, or "an evening of art and laughter," while Sangeet invites emphasize dance, music, and performances — sometimes even asking guests to "come ready to dance."
